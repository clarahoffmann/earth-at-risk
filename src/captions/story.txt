This image shows the destruction in Altadena, Los Angeles County, US, during the Eaton fire in January 2025. The fire started on 7 January and is estimated to have destroyed 9,419 buildings. According to an official investigation, a spark from an out-of-service transmission tower (circle) ignited the fire, and dry desert winds quickly spread it.

The destroyed buildings are colored by the destruction time, estimated from Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) images and one optical image. Most buildings appear destroyed within 8 (yellow) and 24 (orange) hours after ignition, captured by the first two SAR images. Only a few buildings were destroyed later (red), potentially due to different fire exposure, later collapse or detection errors. Unlike optical satellites, SAR satellites see through smoke and in the dark. They are well-suited to monitor destruction while the fire is active. The power lines appear as rainbow-colored streaks because the image is colored by viewing angle and thin wires reflect only from certain angles.

A classifier trained on post-fire images and fire damage inspections predicts the probability of destruction for each building over time. A change point model then uses the probabilities to determine the most likely time of destruction. Infrared satellite data refines this estimate.

The Eaton fire ranks among the most destructive utility-caused fires since 2007, as shown in the bottom bar chart. Power grids near vegetation in dry climates not only pose a fire risk in California but also worldwide. The bottom right map marks such areas in red.
