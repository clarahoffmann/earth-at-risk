This image shows the destruction in Altadena, Los Angeles County, US, during the Eaton fire in January 2025. The fire started on 7 January and is estimated to have destroyed 9,419 buildings. According to an official investigation, a spark from an out-of-service transmission tower (circle) ignited the fire, and dry desert winds quickly spread it.

The destroyed buildings are colored by the estimated destruction time. Most buildings were destroyed between 8 and 24 hours after ignition. To estimate when each building was destroyed, eight SAR images from Capella Space and Umbra and one optical image from Vantor are combined. Unlike optical satellites, Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) satellites see through smoke and in the dark. They are well-suited to monitor destruction while the fire is still active. The power lines at the top appear as rainbow colored streaks because the image is colored by viewing angle and thin wires reflect strongly from certain angles.

A classifier trained on post-fire images and fire damage inspections predicts the probability of destruction for each building over time. A change point model is then fitted to these probabilities to determine the most likely time of destruction. Fire detections from infrared satellites improve this estimate.

The Eaton fire is one of the most destructive utility-caused fires since 2007, according to the bottom bar chart. The combination of dry climate, vegetation and human infrastructure is not unique to California. In the bottom map, red marks regions worldwide where power grids, vegetation and dry climate coincide.
